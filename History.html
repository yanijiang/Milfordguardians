History

The Beginning

Milford Sound was discovered a long time ago when New Zealand was still covered in bushes, forests, seas and lakes. Māori people living in the South Island discovered Milford Sound more than 1000 years ago. Tribes would travel there to fish, hunt, and gather around the fiord and collect precious pounamu. In Maori legend, Milford Sound was formed by Tu-te-raki-whanoa. He was a godly figure who was in charge of shaping the Fiordland coast. The Māori name for Milford Sound, Piopiotahi, means “a single piopio”.

European discovery

Early European settlers did not explore Milford Sound by boat, not realising that inside its narrow entrance there was such a beautiful region to explore. In 1823 a settler called John Grono was the first European settler to visit, and he named it Milford sound after Milford Haven on the welsh coast.

A little while later, a Scotsman called Donald Sutherland became Milford Sound's first permanent resident. In 1871 he left everthing and ailed down the Fiordland coastline, with only his dog for company. When he reached Milford Sound, he decided to stay. Sutherland chose a spot to live near what is now Lady Bowen Falls. He also discovered Sutherland Falls which were named after him. In 1890 he married and his wife joined him in Milford Sound. Together, the Sutherlands built the first hotel in Milford Sound, to accommodate the growing number of walkers arriving there on what is now the Milford Track. After Donald died in 1919, Elizabeth sold the hotel to the government.

Geological History

Milford Sound was formed was formed by glacier erosion over millions of years, where ancient glaciers carved U shaped valleys that were later flooded by the seas as they retreated. In the image you can see the passage of the glaciers over time in the shape and height of the cliffs that rise out of the sea. As years went by and the glaciers melted the thickness of the ice changed, resulting in the ridged walls and U-shaped glacial valleys you can see today. Milford sound is also home to many animals.
