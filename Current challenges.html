Current Challenges

Tourism Impact

Wildlife disturbance

Wildlife disturbance is a significant concern because of high levels on tourism in Miford sound. These are often vulnerable species and interactions with human activities can have bad impacts on their health. Some affected species include:

  • Bottlenose Dolphins: Research indicates that boat traffic significantly affects dolphin behavior. Dolphins in Milford Sound like to avoid boats, leading to less time spent feeding and resting in preferred areas. There have also been some cases of boat strikes which cause injury and even death to dolphins.

  • Fiordland Crested Penguins (Tawaki): These are rare penguins with a small population. They are particularly vulnerable to disturbance during their breeding season (which is from July to November), when they come ashore to nest on beaches. Tourism/human presence can disrupt nesting behavior and could potentially scare adults away from their eggs or chicks.

  • Native Birds: Increased tourism and human activities and noise can startle birds, disrupt their feeding patterns, and interfere with nesting. For example the Kea are a protected species, and their natural behaviors can be altered by human interaction, including the temptation of unnatural food sources.

Waste and litter

Increased tourism leads to more waste and litter. Litter can have a big impact on Milford sound. It can affect wildlife and species, as they might ingest waste products and get sick. It can also lead to water pollution, land pollution and visual pollution, which can have negative affects on ecosystems and the beautiful NZ environment.

Environmental impacts

Carbon emissions and climate change

Carbon emissions have a significant impact on Milford sound. High road usage and water vehicles contribute to carbon emissions, which can lead to global warming and climate change. Climate change has led to increased rainfall and harsh weather conditions, which has led to flooding, slips and severly damaged huts and bridges that happen at Milford Sound. For example, on Februrary 2020 Milford sound recieved 566mmof rain in a single day, much more than the normal amount for the entirety of Februrary, 455mm. This closed down many walks around Milford Sound due to damages infrastructure and landslides.

Introduced Species

Introduced species have a significant impact on Milford Sound, disrupting the delicate balance of its ecosystem. Rats, stoats, and possums pose a major threat to native wildlife, preying on birds, eggs, and insects, which are essential for the biodiversity of Milford Sounds environment. These invasive animals not only compete with native species for food and habitat but also contribute to the decline of vulnerable bird species.
